See what fills your Mac. Choose what to clear.

Find large folders, app caches and build files without knowing where to look. ZerX scans accessible files on your startup disk, shows where space is used and lets you review eligible items before moving them to the Trash.

Download on the Mac App Store

Available now · Free · macOS 13 or later · Apple Silicon and Intel

Understand where your storage goes

  • Find overlooked files

    Explore large folders, app caches, logs and developer files such as build outputs and simulator data. Scan time and coverage depend on your disk, file count and permissions.

  • Trash by default

    Items move to the Trash by default and can be restored in Finder until you empty it. Permanent deletion is a separate setting, off by default, and requires confirmation; it cannot be undone. See the protected folders and exceptions.

  • Extra care with active projects

    When ZerX identifies an active project from recent changes, its caches and build outputs are not preselected. Review warnings before removing files you may still need.

  • Local scanning, no account

    The App Store app does not send file names, contents or scan results to the developer. It has no analytics or account. Installation and updates are handled by Apple.

How it works

  1. Select your startup disk

    Select your startup disk in the standard macOS file picker. ZerX scans files your account can read and reports unreadable areas instead of treating them as empty. No administrator privileges are required to scan. Full Disk Access is optional: it can include more protected files and reduce separate permission prompts, but does not remove every macOS restriction.

  2. Scan

    See a disk-space overview and explore accessible folders. Unreadable locations remain marked; a scan cannot enumerate every protected file.

  3. Review and tick

    Review suggested cleanup items and choose what you no longer need. Personal documents are not automatically selected for cleanup; size alone does not make a file disposable. A cache label is not a guarantee that a file is disposable.

  4. Delete to Trash

    Empty the Trash when you're sure.

Checks before deletion

  • Scope allowlist

    Selecting the startup disk allows inspection beyond your home folder, but deletion is limited to eligible items inside your home folder. System files and other users’ folders are outside that deletion scope.

  • Hard blocklist

    Listed locations for credentials, mail, cloud storage and libraries are protected. Some app caches and device backups are exceptions that you choose manually. Read the specific rules and exceptions before deleting.

  • One gate

    Every deletion passes through a single checkpoint that normalizes the path and re-checks it after resolving symlinks. A symlink into a protected folder doesn't get through. Covered by tests.

FAQ

Will there be a version outside the App Store?

The current public release is available through the Mac App Store. There is no direct download offered on this website.

Is it safe?

ZerX applies deletion restrictions and uses the Trash by default. These checks cannot decide whether every file matters to you: review your selection and keep backups. Read the rules and exceptions on the Safety page.

What access does the app ask for?

Select your startup disk in the standard macOS file picker. ZerX scans files your account can read and reports unreadable areas instead of treating them as empty. No administrator privileges are required to scan. Full Disk Access is optional: it can include more protected files and reduce separate permission prompts, but does not remove every macOS restriction.

Can it clean "System Data" from Settings → Storage?

ZerX helps find accessible caches and logs that may contribute to System Data. The App Store version cannot clean the entire category or delete Time Machine snapshots. macOS may report storage differently.

Does it cost anything?

ZerX 0.1.6 is free on the Mac App Store.

How do I uninstall?

Quit ZerX and move ZerX.app to the Trash in Finder. Local settings may remain in its macOS container; see the privacy policy.
